Chinese checkers strategy

Six tips for the star board, each with a picture from the real rules: ladders, stragglers, the middle, filling your goal, and blocking jumps.

Play now

What these tips rest on

The tips follow from the rules of Chinese checkers as Leapstone plays them: one move is one step or a chain of jumps, and you can jump over a piece of any color. They are not a proof of best play. Playing against the computer is the quickest way to try them.

1. Build ladders

A ladder is a line of pieces with one empty spot between each of them. The pieces can be yours or not (can you jump your own piece?). A piece at one end jumps over the whole line in a single move, so it crosses the board in one turn instead of one spot per turn.

A jump works in all six directions, also backwards (can you move backwards?), so a ladder does not have to start in your own point. Any line of pieces with gaps between them will do.

2. Keep your pieces together

You need all 10 pieces in your goal, so your last piece decides when you finish. A piece with no piece next to it has nothing to jump over, and can only step.

From the tip of your own point to the tip of the goal across the board is 16 steps. That is 16 turns for one piece on its own, while a ladder crosses in one. Move the pieces at the back while there are still pieces around them to jump over. The computer in Leapstone does this too: it scores moves of its rearmost pieces a little higher.

3. Use the middle

A piece in the middle of the board has six neighbors, so it can step or jump in all six directions. A piece at the edge has fewer. The piece at the edge in the picture has four.

Pieces in the middle also give the other pieces something to jump over on their way across.

4. Fill the goal from the back

Your goal is a triangle of 10 spots, in rows of 4, 3, 2 and 1. The spots at the back can only be reached through the spots in front of them. If you fill the front first, the last piece may have no way in.

Put the first pieces into the back spots and work toward the front, so the front stays open for the pieces that arrive last.

5. Do not wall in a piece in your goal

You may stop in the point of another player, so a piece of the other player can end up in your goal. You win only when all 10 spots of your goal hold your own pieces. If that piece has no move, nobody can move it, and you cannot win until a spot next to it opens.

Keep a spot next to such a piece open, so it can leave. The computer in Leapstone counts this: a move that traps a piece of another player in its goal scores lower. The rules do not make anyone leave your goal, so the other player can also park a piece there on purpose.

6. Block a jump, or leave it

A jump needs an empty spot to land on. Fill that spot and the jump is gone. Two pieces in a row cannot be jumped either.

Your pieces are stepping stones for the other player too. In the left picture blue crosses two red pieces in one move. On the right, red moved one piece into the first landing spot, and blue can only step.

A move spent blocking is a move not spent on your own progress, so block when the jump gives the other player more than your move would give you. It works the other way round when you build a ladder: after your move, the other player moves first, so a piece of theirs at one end uses your ladder before you do. On the hardest level, the computer looks at the best reply of each opponent before it chooses a move.
